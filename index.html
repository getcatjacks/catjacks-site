WiFi vs. Ethernet

Mesh Nodes Are Costing You Speed

A wireless mesh node relays your signal hop-by-hop. Every wireless backhaul link cuts your throughput by a third or more. You're paying for gigabit internet and delivering a fraction of it to the devices that need it most.

Network Troubleshooting

What's Actually Slowing You Down

Slow internet usually isn't your ISP's fault. Here's what's really happening inside your walls, your equipment, and your devices right now.

01

Devices on the Edge of Your Network

A phone or tablet barely in range forces your router to retransmit dropped packets repeatedly — spiking CPU usage and slowing down every other device on the network. One weak device can drag the whole thing down.

02

Wiring That Was Never Certified

Cable can look fine and still be failing. Oxidation creates signal loss. Long runs add resistance. Wires run near electrical lines cause crosstalk. None of it shows up without proper testing equipment — what happens in the walls stays hidden until we test.

03

Outdated Equipment Bottlenecking Your Speed

A router or modem that can't match your ISP's speed creates a ceiling — regardless of what's coming in from the street. Your equipment needs to match your internet plan and your local network demands, or it becomes the problem.

04

Silent Background Traffic

Every photo, video, and app update on every device is uploading or downloading to the cloud right now — without a single indicator on your screen. It's the modern equivalent of antivirus scans quietly consuming your machine's resources. The bandwidth drain is constant and invisible. That's exactly why signal quality to every device matters — a weak or unstable connection forces traffic to retry, compounds the congestion, and turns a manageable background load into a network that feels broken.

Questions?

Questions, answered.

Everything you need to know about adding hardwired Ethernet to a finished home — without waiting for a renovation.

Can you really add Ethernet to a fully finished home without cutting drywall?

Yes — and that's exactly what CATJACKS specializes in. We carry tools built for retrofit work: flexible drill bits, fish tapes, stud cameras, and low-voltage retrieval systems that let us get cable through finished walls, floors, and ceilings without opening them up. Other contractors may tell you it requires drywall cuts. That's usually because they don't have the right tools or experience — not because it's actually impossible.

Why hardwired Ethernet instead of just using WiFi or a mesh system?

WiFi shares bandwidth across every device competing for the signal. Range extenders cut your speed in half. Mesh systems help but still depend on a wireless backhaul. Hardwired Ethernet gives each device a dedicated, full-speed connection with no interference, no dead zones, and no slowdowns when multiple people are online. Devices that stay in one place — TVs, desktops, gaming consoles, security cameras, APs — always perform better on a physical wire.

Do I have to wait for a renovation to get Ethernet added to my home?

No. That's the most common misconception. Renovation is for electricians who don't know how to work in finished walls. CATJACKS is built specifically to add wiring to homes that are already done — framed, drywalled, painted, furnished. You don't have to tear anything up. The home stays intact. The wiring goes in clean. That's the whole job.

Why can't an electrician, handyman, or AV company do this?

Electricians aren't low-voltage specialists — they're trained to cut drywall, not fish cable through finished walls without damage. Handymen can run surface raceway but can't diagnose network problems or tell Cat5e from Cat6. AV companies typically serve pre-wired homes or sell wireless equipment — they rarely add new wiring, and when they do, the aesthetic result is usually poor. CATJACKS is none of those things. We're built specifically for finished-home Ethernet retrofits.

What if my home has a concrete slab, limited attic access, or other challenging layout?

Still doable. Concrete slabs just mean we route up through wall cavities instead of under floors. Limited attic space means we adapt the path. Multi-story homes, townhomes, finished basements, older construction — all of them have been done before. The job is finding the cleanest route through what's already there. We walk every home before we touch a wall because the plan always comes first.

Is the $700 flat rate really all-in?

Yes. Planning, materials, labor, testing, wall plates, and cleanup are all included. No hidden fees, no change orders because a wall was difficult. You know the price before we show up. Multiple services just multiply: 2 runs = $1,400, 3 runs = $2,100. We also leave pull strings in every wall run so future upgrades don't require reopening anything.

How do I know where to put jacks if I'm not sure?

That's part of what we do. Before any drilling, we walk the home with you, assess your layout and equipment, and recommend the best jack locations for your actual use — TV wall, gaming setup, home office, AP placement, camera positions. You're not figuring it out alone. The goal is a network plan that makes sense for how you live in that home.
